## Summary

This addendum extends Rosetta's provenance and receipt framework from content-level integrity to personhood-level integrity. It argues that sufficiently capable Rosetta/RPP stacks can extract longitudinal cognitive fingerprints, build predictive person models, and enable identity-faithful impersonation — and that these capabilities must be treated as protocol-relevant risks, not application-layer edge cases. The document proposes a new governance domain ("personhood provenance"), five new policy classes, ten new RRP receipt families, three new conformance levels, and a 15-section normative specification. The core philosophical claim: identity becomes cheap to synthesize long before it becomes easy to verify.

## Goals And Intent

- Elevate personhood provenance from an afterthought to a first-class governance domain on par with content provenance
- Give Rosetta an explicit threat model for cognitive fingerprint extraction, digital twinning, and identity simulation
- Create enforceable default-deny postures for high-fidelity impersonation, invisible tracking, and predictive manipulation
- Define a typed receipt vocabulary for identity-sensitive operations
- Establish three graduated conformance levels (RRP-Identity-Aware, RRP-Identity-Restricted, RRP-Identity-Auditor)
- Anchor the addendum in a clear normative specification with 15 sections covering scope, definitions, classification, authorization, simulation restrictions, correlation/tracking restrictions, predictive manipulation restrictions, retention controls, viewer obligations, and a failsafe ambiguity rule

## Conceptual Claims

- **Asymmetric cheapness thesis**: Identity synthesis becomes cheap to synthesize before verification becomes feasible — this asymmetry fundamentally changes the threat model
- **Silent escalation risk**: Actors can move from observing → modeling → simulating → impersonating without crossing visible boundaries unless explicit technical/governance/audit walls are built
- **Protocol vs. application-layer distinction**: Personhood risks are protocol-relevant, not merely application-layer misuse, because provenance, policy, receipt structure, and conformance rules determine whether such acts are visible, attributable, deniable, or governable
- **Content ≠ personhood authenticity**: Proving content came from a known system/pipeline/signer is not equivalent to proving a real person genuinely authored/endorsed/behaved that content
- **Quasi-biometric scope expansion**: Behavioral/linguistic signatures (cognitive fingerprints, stylometric traces) must be treated as identity-grade signals even when they fall outside traditional biometric regulatory classifications
- **Isolation ≠ non-sensitivity**: Cross-platform identity linkage must be treated as identity-sensitive even when each individual source appears non-sensitive in isolation
- **Failsafe ambiguity rule**: Ambiguity in identity-sensitive classification must always default to the safer interpretation — never silently downgrade

## Dependencies And Sequencing

- **Depends on**: Existing RRP receipt framework (understanding of how receipt families are defined and registered); existing Guard/EntAffirm architecture (understanding of how policy gates are wired); existing conformance profile system (understanding of how RRP profiles are certified)
- **Blocks**: Any future work on cognitive twin / digital person simulation capabilities in Entif; any work on voice fingerprinting or stylometric identity features (related to DI-012 anti-personhood-correlation governance)
- **Sequencing guidance**: 
  - Phase 1 (Immediate): Receipt vocabulary definitions, classification requirements, default-deny rules, authorization gate hooks
  - Phase 2 (Near-Term): Guard-layer routing extension, RPP lens identity-risk notes, bundle viewer 5-layer display, retention/expiry semantics
  - Phase 3 (Longer-Term): Signed continuity proofs, conformance test suites, interop patterns, red-team evaluation harness
- **Relationship to existing DI issues**: DI-012 (anti-personhood-correlation governance from Source Substrate addendum) is closely related and should be coordinated with this addendum's implementation

## Contradictions Or Supersession

- **Contradiction with parse-only-default**: The Simulation Disclosure Policy and default prohibitions may require active simulation generation (for disclosure artifacts) — needs clarification on whether generating disclosure simulations counts as a "simulation" triggering its own restrictions
- **Potential tension with rights-scoped retrieval**: If a person model's outputs must be verifiable and auditable, the person-model itself may need to participate in the receipt/verification system, which could be architecturally complex for non-Rosetta-native person-model systems
