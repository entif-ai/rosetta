## Summary

This document codifies a consensus-first, right-sized commitment framework for agentic systems and human-AI collaboration. It defines five required artifacts (Opportunity & Edge Brief, Commitment Canvas, Evidence Ledger, Falsification & Dissent Packet, Exit/Pivot/Salvage Plan), a phase-transition handshake protocol, a wager-sizing scoring rubric, and AI-specific safeguards against hallucination and miscalibration. It references Lean Startup, Stage-Gate, Discovery-Driven Planning, real-options theory, premortem techniques, and psychological safety research as intellectual foundations.

## Goals And Intent

- Eliminate premature closure and "spec by fluent synthesis" failure modes
- Replace "cheapest next move" with minimum-necessary commitment given opportunity magnitude, edge durability, downside tolerance, reversibility, and competitive tempo
- Embed dissent, provenance, and exit logic as first-class design objects
- Counter motivated reasoning, automation bias, and LLM overconfidence in commitment decisions

## Components And Technologies

- Five required artifacts: Opportunity & Edge Brief, Commitment Canvas, Evidence Ledger, Falsification & Dissent Packet, Exit/Pivot/Salvage Plan
- Phase-transition handshake protocol with four mandatory questions
- Commitment calibration loop (continuous)
- Wager-sizing scoring rubric (Low/Medium/High per dimension, plain-English decision rule)
- Anti-motivated reasoning guardrail set (four mechanisms)
- AI-specific safeguards (hypothesis labeling, confidence bounding, SME prompt)
- Spec header with commitment posture tracker (9 states)
- Fake door / painted door experiment writeup template
- Premortem writeup template

## Conceptual Claims

- Premature closure is amplified by motivated reasoning; synthesis is not readiness
- Automation bias in human-AI collaboration requires explicit counter-measures
- LLM overconfidence and hallucination become operationally lethal when translated into commitments without evidence management
- Groupthink means "consensus" must mean understanding the strongest informed objection
- "Cheapest next move" should be replaced with minimum-necessary commitment given the pot
- Real-options theory (expand/delay/abandon) is the correct economic frame for commitment sizing
- Dissent is a protected channel; psychological safety enables the error-reporting necessary for correct decisions
- Evidence quality must drive confidence levels, not the model's fluency
- "Spec by fluent synthesis" is explicitly forbidden in AI-assisted workflows

## Dependencies And Sequencing

- Discovery-Driven Planning (Ries) — assumption-based milestone funding
- Real Options (Copeland/Keen) — flexibility-under-uncertainty framing
- Premortem (Klein) — failure assumption debiasing
- Devil's advocacy / Team A/Team B — structured contrarian challenge
- Psychological safety (Edmondson) — interpersonal risk-taking for learning
- Stage-Gate (Cooper) — vocabulary and structure (not the default frame)
- Lean Startup (Ries) — MVP as learning vehicle (not "always go cheapest")

## Contradictions Or Supersession

- The framework explicitly rejects "always go cheapest" from Lean Startup interpretation in favor of minimum-necessary given the pot — distinguishing between cheap-first and right-sized
- Stage-Gate is acknowledged as vocabulary/structure useful in many environments but not the default frame for high-uncertainty ventures; Discovery-Driven Planning is the preferred alternative
- User preferences are signal (not scripture) — this directly contradicts treating survey results as authoritative commitment inputs
