## Problem Statement

Entif's GTM motion should NOT be "sell the universal brain-governance engine." That pitch is too large, too diffuse, and too risky for enterprise procurement. The correct near-term sale is a scoped design partner engagement around one concrete workflow wedge.

## Recommended Sale

**"A design-partner engagement around governed enterprise AI routing, privacy membrane, semantic caching, and auditable orchestration"**

This is NOT a commitment to the full platform. It is a scoped engagement that:
- Has a fixed term
- Includes an architecture workshop and pilot scoping
- Produces a commercial conversion path into broader deployment
- Gives the design partner priority access and direct roadmap influence

## Three-Track Parallel Approach

### Track 1: Belief Pack (Artifact Motion)
- 10-12 slide deck
- 1-page executive memo
- 1-page security/governance posture
- 1-page design partner offer
- 1-page ROI frame: token savings, privacy boundary reduction, governance simplification

### Track 2: Commercial Motion (Sales)
- Leverage warm network: ex-colleagues who are C-level/SVPs at Fortune 100s and 9-figure ARR startups
- "Warm-intro detonation" not cold prospecting
- Paid design partner structure
- Fixed term + architecture workshop + pilot scoping → commercial conversion

### Track 3: Build Track (Engineering)
- MVP that serves the sales motion
- Demo-ability is the primary constraint
- One concrete workflow wedge first (e.g., internal policy/benefits/handbook workflow)
- NOT the full cathedral

## The Wedge Is the Point

The conversation makes clear: "enterprises do not buy 'all your problems solved.' They buy 'this one expensive, scary problem stops hurting on day 30.'"

The handbook/benefits workflow is specifically called out as the ideal first wedge because it is:
- Measurable: show lower latency, lower cost, better auditability in one shot
- Safe: policy/benefits errors are consequential but not catastrophic
- Legible: non-technical stakeholders can understand the value
- Bounded: scope is clear, not open-ended

## $100M ARR Reality Check

The $100M ARR by month six projection is explicitly challenged as "champagne hallucination." Enterprise sales cycles are slow, procurement is slow, regulated industry security review is rigorous. The realistic path: dominate one specific wedge, prove measurable savings + risk reduction, then scale from evidence.
