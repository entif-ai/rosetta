## Summary

C2PA (Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity) and Content Credentials provide cryptographic media provenance for content — a necessary but insufficient foundation for identity assurance. C2PA addresses "was this content generated by a known system?" but not "does this content represent a genuine living person's authorship, endorsement, or behavioral continuity?" Rosetta's personhood provenance requirements extend beyond what C2PA covers. This gap must be explicitly documented and addressed.

## What C2PA Does

C2PA (ISO 21024-1) provides:
- Cryptographic signing of content provenance (device → software → platform)
- Content authenticity: "this image/video was taken by Camera X, processed by App Y"
- Tamper-evident metadata for media files
- Content Credentials: transparent history of content creation/modification

C2PA scope: **content provenance**, not **personhood provenance**.

## What C2PA Does NOT Do

- Does NOT verify whether a person genuinely authored, endorsed, or is connected to the content
- Does NOT address behavioral or linguistic fingerprints (voice, writing style, discourse patterns)
- Does NOT provide continuity proofs for a person's digital presence over time
- Does NOT address cognitive twin or predictive person-model scenarios
- Does NOT prevent high-fidelity impersonation of a real person's style within C2PA-signed content
- Does NOT address cross-platform identity correlation

## Research and Integration Path

2. **Evaluate C2PA as one input to identity-assurance stack** — C2PA-signed content provides one signal in a multi-signal identity-assurance system
3. **Assess C2PA adoption trajectory** — C2PA adoption is growing (Adobe, Microsoft, Google); Rosetta should design for C2PA interoperability while supplementing where C2PA is insufficient
4. **Prototype C2PA + RPP-Identity layered approach** — Show how C2PA content provenance + RPP personhood provenance compose together
