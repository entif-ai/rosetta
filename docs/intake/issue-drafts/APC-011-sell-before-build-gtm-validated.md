## Problem Statement

The conversation validates that for a thing with enough architectural depth already on paper, **the market motion does not need to wait for the demo.** However, the specific GTM structure matters enormously.

The wrong GTM: "Entif, the universal brain-governance engine for all enterprise AI."

The right GTM: "a design-partner engagement around governed enterprise AI routing, privacy membrane, semantic caching, and auditable orchestration."

The latter is not asking buyers to purchase finished software. It is asking them to buy: priority access, direct roadmap influence, a pilot architecture shaped to their environment, and first crack at the cost/risk advantage.

## The Three-Track GTM Structure (from Source)

### Track 1: The Belief Pack (Artifacts)

The "big shop" artifact stack:
- 10–12 slide deck (market problem, Entif solution, category, proof points)
- 1-page executive memo (the single-page version for time-constrained C-suite)
- 1-page security/governance posture (what Entif does and doesn't do with data)
- 1-page design partner offer (what design partners get, timeline, commitments)
- 1-page ROI frame (token savings, privacy boundary reduction, governance simplification)

### Track 2: The Commercial Motion (Warm Network)

Not cold prospecting — the network is warm (ex-colleagues who became C-level/SVPs).

The commercial offer:
- Paid design partner (fixed term engagement)
- Architecture workshop + pilot scoping (on-site or remote)
- First implementation lane in one constrained workflow (handbook, benefits, etc.)
- Commercial conversion path into broader deployment

This matches the "sell pilots before SaaS" pattern already in Crates's playbook.

### Track 3: The Build Track

Keep building the MVP but let it serve the sales motion:
- The v0 acceptance criteria define what "production-worthy pilot" means
- Each build milestone becomes demo ammunition and procurement sedative
- Build in thin vertical slices that are individually impressive and saleable

## The Network Advantage

The conversation notes that Crates has contacts at the uppermost echelons:
- Ex-colleagues whose careers were "catapulted to great heights" thanks to Crates's contributions
- These people wrote "stunning, glowing references" before parting ways
- They are now C-level at 9-figure ARR startups and SVPs at Fortune 100s

The GTM challenge is not "how to reach buyers" — it's "how to package this so the right killer closes it fast."

Commission-driven closers (seven-figure commissions for two months of prospecting) are motivated by:
1. A live demo of solved pain with obvious ROI
2. A product that closes before the commission window expires

## The Specific First Sale

The exact offer to make:
> "Here is an internal policy/benefits/handbook workflow. Here is what happens when employees ask this naively through generic AI. Here is what happens when the same request goes through Entif. Here is what stayed on-prem. Here is what was normalized. Here is what was cached. Here is what was audited. Here is how much cheaper it was. Here is the proof."

This is not selling a moonshot. It is selling a weapon with a commission attached.
